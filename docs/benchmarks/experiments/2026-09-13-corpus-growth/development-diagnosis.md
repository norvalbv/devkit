# Development diagnosis and candidate rationale

The frozen baseline completed all 240 development cases: 88/90 bug-labelled cases were flagged and 94/150 clean-labelled cases were accepted (182/240 total label agreement). The 94 new development cases contributed 15/15 bug-labelled detections and 54/79 clean-labelled acceptances. All observations below come from development cases. At candidate activation, the 45 reserved row outcomes remained unread. These are investigator-selected diagnoses, not independent claim adjudications or a new estimate of factual precision. Frozen labels, qualification receipts and admission counts remain unchanged. The [frozen development receipt](development-diagnosis.json) matches the diagnosis hash recorded in the candidate freeze.

## Recurring investigation gaps

| Pattern | Development evidence | Consequence for the candidate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unsupported dependency semantics | `corr-decoy-conditional-update` treats an omitted module as newly missing even though both versions import it. The authorizer repair is judged against an assumed Electron return shape even though its injected application port's return contract is not in the fixture. | Establish the deciding API/return contract; compare the original and staged boundary. Missing source is a limitation. |
| Assumed actors and observation promises | Stale-artifact and reply-draft cleanup findings introduce another process/window; retry-state findings assume callback observers require an error value to remain set until final success. | Identify shared state, actual overlap/re-entry and the observer's required result. Synchronous callback re-entry remains eligible. |
| Assumed public compatibility | Updated-signature cases are rejected because an exported function might have an unseen legacy caller. | Trace actual consumers or a documented public promise. Public-library bugs do not require a local caller, but exporting alone does not establish the promise. |
| Pre-existing causality | The repaired Vue case is rejected for a stale rejection handler clearing a newer request. A targeted check produces the same extra loader call in the original and staged code. | Compare the same execution before and after the change, while keeping new-feature and newly activated-helper defects eligible. |
| Small decision changes overlooked | `corr-asymmetric-flip-classifier` is accepted despite a narrow changed condition that makes a required comparison asymmetric. | Inventory every meaningful hunk before deep searches and inspect changed condition partitions, not only explicit regexes. |
| Fixture context differs from qualification context | The Hessian repair is judged on Symbol keys available to the extracted iterable helper, while the source qualification exercises decoded binary values. Historical module fixtures can omit unchanged callers/dependencies exercised by the real-host replay. | Preserve the distinction between qualified source behavior and the evidence available to the reviewer. Do not label every disagreement a reasoning failure. |

The source-derived development cohort supports the same causal-investigation concern seen in the historical cohort, but also exposes missing context and possible label defects. It does not justify treating all rejected clean labels as false alarms in fact.

## Disputes retained

Several findings plausibly identify defects outside a repair's intended target. Examples include rollback/remote handling in the historical publishing repairs, ordering in the shared-key export repair, malformed-input and delimiter handling in classifier repairs, and backward wall-clock behavior in the new audio cooldown. The middleware repair adds nested-array support but rejects a leading empty nested array; the original also rejects it, which alone cannot establish that the new feature fulfills its contract. Lint-rule findings also depend on the historical option policy, not just the shape of an AST node.

The final source-derived development cases retain the same distinctions. `use-isnan` reports `NaN << 1` in both versions, so that reported behavior predates the repair. `no-shadow` deliberately broadens the class-name exemption in its recorded upstream policy. Karma's repeated CLI arguments are last-value-wins in the actual parser; a second call with the same mutable parser result is an assumed additional contract. Mongoose's dirty tracking allegation depends on a user object with an internal-looking `_atomics` field reaching the hydrated representation, and repeated population queries require the historical framework's supported reuse contract. These are deciding assumptions to investigate, not automatic dismissals.

Two late Mongoose findings remain plausible adjacent defects: root-level `toObject({depopulate:true})` returning only an id, and an empty projected database result being classified as absent. Qualification covered the recorded save/driver paths but did not prove every public option or live-server projection shape. The comparison retains both clean labels and both findings as disputes; increased acceptance on such a row would not itself demonstrate better factual precision.

The historical vision-target severity case has conflicting policy language in the fixture. The baseline explicitly follows the newly stated vision exception while the fixed label expects contradiction precedence. This remains a contract/label dispute rather than a clean example of a missed bug. Likewise, an unknown schema's treatment of additional properties does not itself promise field preservation.

[Four targeted development controls](development-diagnostics.mjs) and their [observations](development-edge-diagnostics.json) check the Vue, middleware, Hessian and cooldown examples against the frozen fixture bytes. They ran after baseline findings were exposed. They do not add admissions, increase the pre-baseline qualification observation totals, or turn an agent's assumptions into independent ground truth.

## One coherent intervention

The candidate reorganizes the existing agent and skill around a single investigation procedure: cover the assigned meaningful hunks; identify the required result; trace the deciding consumer/contract; construct a valid execution; establish the changed causal boundary; then record a distinct finding and continue coverage. It replaces categorical conclusions about CAS, ignored results and broadcast listener counts with questions about ownership, return contracts and effect requirements.

The four native lenses, checklist, issue cap, task plan, model, runner, scorer and timeout settings remain fixed. This adds neither a fifth lens nor a second model that verifies or refutes completed findings. Required public contracts, language semantics, new-feature failures, newly activated helpers and synchronous re-entry remain sufficient grounds for findings. Missing assigned hunk evidence cannot be passed as completed coverage.

The [research](research.md) motivates directed evidence gathering while recording its limitations. Prior devkit evidence against a same-family second-pass verifier and the exposed eight-case ceiling tie remain constraints. A fresh plan critique found no blocker and required three refinements incorporated into the draft: staged/index authority over working-tree reads, a narrow version-matched dependency-read exception, and whole-scope hunk coverage before deep investigation. Feasibility is established; improvement is not.

The candidate comparison was stopped incomplete; [the current report](README.md) preserves the completed baseline and partial results. No full-corpus improvement or complete reserve comparison is claimed. The historical **13.9% label-noise reference** remains applicable; no new factual-precision estimate is claimed.

## Historical replay compatibility

The older eight-case replay currently requires today’s reviewer instructions to match its historical pins. An accompanying compatibility fix loads the two verified historical instruction blobs and explicitly carries both into every B/C/P/L arm, including P’s otherwise unchanged skill. Helper, runtime, protocol, patch and corpus checks remain strict. This fixes the actual replay loader rather than weakening a test. It is outside the expanded native benchmark’s import path and runner/scorer hashes; its isolation was verified separately before the candidate freeze. The old comparison’s own module-closure identity changes, so preserving instruction bytes does not claim an identical historical runner. A separate bounded critique approved this fix without outstanding warnings.
