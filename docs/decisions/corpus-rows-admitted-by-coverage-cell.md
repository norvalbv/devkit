# corpus-rows-admitted-by-coverage-cell

## Target · 2026-09-02 — Corpus rows are admitted by coverage cell and audited as computed properties; demotion only on current-epoch evidence

**Context:** The reviewer corpus grew by supply — every mined or authored row was appended — with no instrument saying whether a row carried information: 70 of the 90 twice-measured correctness rows were always-correct, 44 of 237 rows had never been measured, 25 of 50 minimal pairs straddled the holdout boundary so the holdout scored 8.5 points EASIER than dev, three of five suites sat under the documented ≥3-holdout-per-class floor with nothing checking it at load, the tracker compared deltas against a noise floor hardcoded to 0, and no published metric could tell 'learned the boundary' (both members of a pair correct, 66%) from 'right on one side' (the 81% marginal). A 503-row queue waited to be appended into that instrument.
**Ruling:** Row value is a COMPUTED property, printed by the harness, not assumed: a read-only audit (reviewers/audit.mts) reports never-measured, constant-correct, always-wrong and flipped rows, per-lens gold counts, pair groups (caseId ∪ variantOf, union-find) with an unmeasured member or straddling the holdout, unrelated near-twins (5-shingle Jaccard ≥ 0.5) and the holdout floor per class. Admission runs by coverage need, not supply: finalize --check refuses a proposal that is an unrelated near-twin of an existing row (an intended minimal pair — shared caseId or variantOf — is exempt) and prints the coverage cell the row fills. Holdout is assigned per pair GROUP, never per row, and loadRows warns (DEVKIT_HOLDOUT_FLOOR_STRICT=1 refuses) when a class is under the floor. Pair-level contrast consistency is published beside the marginal; every reviewer ratio carries the measured label-noise floor (the Wilson upper bound, 13.9%, until a second κ round); mined candidates carry the label rule that minted them. Demotion of constant-correct rows to a tripwire tier happens ONLY on observations from the current epoch, after the re-baseline (sc-2494) — never on the sonnet/haiku 2026-08 checkpoints.
**Consequences:**
- Positive: A corpus that grows only where a cell is thin, with a printed reason per admission; a holdout that can detect overfitting because no near-copy of a holdout row sits in dev; a delta that is 'flat' until it clears the measured noise; a pair metric that cannot be inflated by getting one side right; per-tier noise numbers for mined labels instead of one pooled figure; and a saturation report that cannot be mistaken for a retirement list.
- Negative: The Wilson upper bound makes most single-run deltas read 'flat' until the corpus or a second κ round tightens it; near-twin admission uses a fixed 0.5 shingle threshold on normalized fixture text, which can refuse a legitimately similar template (the api-security size-5 mutation cluster would not have been admitted) and cannot see semantic twins with different tokens; reassigning holdout by group moves rows and breaks pairing for every moved row, so it lands inside the re-baseline's epoch break; the strict floor stays a warning until then, so a suite can still run under the floor today.
**Vision-fit:** n/a — internal tooling
**Researched:** 2026-08-31 bench-validity research (Q2 ranked options 2, 3, 5, 8 and the verified saturation / holdout / pair-consistency numbers, all reproduced by audit.mts on 2026-09-02: 12 never-measured, 70/90 constant-correct, 9 always-wrong, 50 pairs / 25 straddling); Land & Bikel 2605.30504 (constant-item filter — used as a report, its 81% mislabel figure explicitly NOT borrowed); Gardner et al. 2004.02709 (contrast-set consistency); methodology items 4 and 19; feature-critique 2026-09-02 (demotion-timing blocker).
**Rejected:** (a) retire constant-correct rows now — REJECTED: every checkpoint is sonnet/haiku from 2026-08-01..02 and story sc-2494 AC#3 forbids retiring on the old model's saturation; (b) a minhash npm dependency for the twin scan — REJECTED: nothing maintained sits in node_modules, and a bench-only helper must not add a runtime dependency to a package that ships inside consumers' node_modules; (c) hard-failing loadRows on the holdout floor today — REJECTED: three suites would refuse to run before the re-baseline fixes their assignment; (d) the 4.2% point estimate as the noise floor — REJECTED: n=48 gives a Wilson interval of [1.2%, 13.9%], and a floor set at the point estimate lets a one-row move publish as a win.

- 2026-09-04 — sc-2494 (2026-09-04) — a baseline-rebuild tool was BUILT AND WITHDRAWN. It reconstructed results.baseline.json from a completed run's printed transcript, for runs invoked without --baseline. Across four review rounds it drew ten distinct correctness findings, each a different way for reconstructed evidence to be laundered or lost: outage rows recorded as ordinary misses, escalated runs crashing instead of refusing, cascade mode misderived, an unreadable output silently overwritten, a lock private to one of two writers, verdicts re-attachable to a moved corpus, duplicate row ids silently overwritten, a stranded lock wedging all future writes. The durable fix belongs in the bench: persist per-row results on completion so nothing ever needs reconstruction.
